# Speed research, 2026-10-03 (revision 2): what the literature and our own record say is left

**Model priority (Hamed, 2026-10-03): 1 DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, 2 MiniMax-M3, 3 GLM-5.3-Flash. Every model gets as much throughput as possible; the work is ordered by this ranking, and
anything built for a lower-ranked model must be ported back to the higher-ranked ones.** Revision 1 of this file covered only MiniMax and GLM; this revision starts from DeepSeek.

Nothing in `src/` changed. Numbers marked *measured* come from our logs, docs or scripts; *estimate* means arithmetic from measured inputs with the measurement that would confirm it;
statements about papers, repositories and model cards are what their abstracts, cards and fetched summaries say (the papers' code was not run, and several cards give no method).
Sources are linked at the end.

## 0. Status update, same day (Hamed's storage decision)

Hamed's priority list put DeepSeek first and allowed removing GLM from the internal SSD. Done, in this order: (1) the internal GLM directory was confirmed to be a pure Hugging Face download (54 files, only the
`.cache/huggingface/download` bookkeeping extra; nothing generated by us inside; our contiguous GLM bank is a separate directory, `~/GLM-5.3-Flash-bank`, left in place); (2) a complete copy already existed at
`/Volumes/X10Pro/models/GLM-5.3-Flash-MLX-4bit-MTP` and was compared byte for byte (53 files, 0 differences); (3) the internal GLM directory (169 GiB) was deleted; (4) DeepSeek's 2-bit bank (142 GiB, 42 files)
was copied back to `~/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-q2g128` and compared byte for byte (0 differences), a marker file `.cachalot-verified` was written, and `serve.sh`/`chat.sh` now use the internal bank when the marker exists
(`serve-glm.sh`/`chat-glm.sh` fall back to the X10Pro copy). Internal free space: 111 GiB. **D1 (tiering) is therefore moot for the full bank; D1b was executed.** What it costs: GLM now reads over USB, an estimated 14 ms a
miss x ~100 misses = ~1.4 s a token (~0.7 tok/s) until a REAP mask or substitution cuts its misses; priority 3, accepted by Hamed.

**First DeepSeek run on the internal bank (smoke test, not a measurement).** Five requests then three repeats of one prompt through `./serve.sh` (52 GiB budget, hotlist 8 GiB): 4.1-5.1 tok/s at a 90-96 % hit rate and
10-24 misses a token, against 9.4-9.6 tok/s in the README's 0.9.x sessions. **Invalid as a speed figure**: memory pressure level 12 (normal is 80+), swap 5.6 of 7 GiB used, the screensaver running, MLX peak 71.7 GiB
(67.7 in the README session), the runtime's resident set 63 GiB. The pressure returned to 84 as soon as the server stopped. Two things worth following up in D0 on a settled machine: the server's own counters showed
**47k predicted expert loads of which 16.8k were used (36 %)**, i.e. ~30k experts (300 GB) read for nothing during 1,661 tokens, and the Engram tables (189 GiB) still sit on the X10Pro (sparse random reads of ~96 small preads a
token over USB).

## 1. Where the time goes, per model

### 1.1 DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (priority 1)

Architecture facts (config, checkpoint, README): 40 layers x 384 routed experts = 15,360, top-6 (240 expert uses a token), 552B parameters, FP8 trunk, FP4 experts (17.93 MiB), our 2-bit affine g128 bank
(9.49 MiB, 142 GiB on disk), Engram tables (196B parameters on disk), hyper-connections, compressed sparse attention. The official checkpoint carries DSpark draft stages (`mtp.0-2`, 2,401 tensors,
§9.1); `src/cachalot/model/dspark_draft.py` implements them.

**Internal-SSD era (measured, README "Performance", 0.9.x).** 52 GiB expert budget = 5,300 slots (34.5 % of the experts), session hit rate 92.3 %, 9.4-9.6 tok/s (105 ms a token). The token is
an **all-resident floor of 77-80 ms** (13.1 tok/s; 56 ms inside `mx.eval`, 21.5 ms of CPU outside) plus **18.5 misses x ~1.7 ms** (1.41 blocked + 0.31 in eval; 1.46 ms raw read at 6.8 GB/s).
Inside the floor: the FP8 trunk's GEMV family 14.0 ms (5.14 GB a token at ~367 GB/s, 79 % of `mx.sum` over the same bytes), routing prediction 11 ms, the 40 per-layer `mx.eval` round trips 9-12 ms,
routed experts 6.9, compressor/indexer 5.5, `wo_a` 4.3, hyper-connection glue 4.2, head/Engram/router 3.2, sparse attention 2.6, Engram reads 1.9. Per layer ~1.4 ms GPU + ~0.55 ms CPU, serialised by the
router `mx.eval` that every MoE layer must run (§9.15).

**The regime changed on 2026-09-30 (0.48.1) and DeepSeek's constants with it.** The internal DeepSeek bank was deleted; `serve.sh` and `chat.sh` now read the bank from the X10Pro over USB (1.0 GB/s wall, queue depth 1).
A miss is 9.95 MB / 1.0 GB/s = ~10 ms, not 1.7: six times dearer. *Estimate* (not measured since 0.48.1): 77 + 18.5 x 10.2 = **~266 ms a token, ~3.8 tok/s**, against 9.4 in the internal era; the one measured
USB figure is a cold 12k prefill at 1,131 s (~11 tok/s, memory pressure 9, contaminated) against ~124-130 s (~100 tok/s) internal. Every DeepSeek closure in the closed list was priced at the internal miss cost
(DSpark 1.03-1.20x, mirror striping, budget curves): they were right for their constants and are stale now.

**Efficiency against the machine (estimate).** Bytes a token: trunk 5.14 GB + 240 experts x 9.95 MB = 2.39 GB = ~7.5 GB. The 77 ms floor streams that at ~98 GB/s, 12 % of the M3 Ultra's 819 GB/s
(`mx.sum` reaches ~465 GB/s on the trunk). antirez's `ds4`, a pure C/Metal engine with no graph framework and the model at 2-bit experts plus 8-bit trunk, decodes DeepSeek V4 Flash at 39.35 tok/s
(25 ms) at 2k context and 27.64 tok/s (36 ms) at 64k on an M5 Max 128 GB (614 GB/s). That is a different, smaller model (V4 Flash, 256 experts x 43 layers, hidden 4096) and a different chip, so it is a
yardstick, not a promise: a 2-3x lower floor than ours exists in a framework-free decode loop. Rapid-MLX (MLX-native, M3 Ultra 256 GB, 213 GB REAP-2bit checkpoint resident) autoregresses at 9.58 tok/s: our
streaming runtime's all-resident floor (13.1 tok/s) is already faster than the MLX-native resident one.

With a two-tier layout (section 4, D1) the cost per miss is `1.7 x (1 - c) + 10.2 x c`, c = the share of misses still read from USB: c = 2 % gives 1.9 ms, 5 % 2.2, 10 % 2.6, 20 % 3.4. At 92.3 % hit and
c = 5 % a token is ~118 ms (8.5 tok/s), **2.25x the USB figure**, with no output change.

## 2. Review of our own process

**Workload-changing levers moved tokens; machine-tuning levers did not, until the loop's structure was the limit.** Of the ~80 levers in the closed list, those that moved a token by more than 10 % changed what the
drive and GPU saw: the contiguous bank, prefetch from the layer's own routing, miss substitution, slot images and bias codes, the memory governor, and for MiniMax the **GPU-side expert selection with the host one layer
behind** (0.31.0: the all-hit floor fell from ~72 to 34 ms because 60 per-layer host round trips left the critical path). Kernel and ordering work landed at 0-5 %.

**A feature-parity gap, and it points at the top-priority model.** The GPU-select loop (0.31.0), miss substitution (0.39-0.43), pair slots and bias codes were built for MiniMax and, in part, GLM. DeepSeek, the
first model and now priority 1, still runs the pre-0.31 structure: 40 `mx.eval` round trips a token (57 ms of wait plus 21-29 ms of CPU gap, §9.15), no substitution, no GPU-side selection. Its closed S1/S1c-style
reasoning (§18.37) was done for GLM's 16 % all-hit layers; at DeepSeek's 92 % hit and six experts, 61 % of layers are all-hit, the regime where MiniMax's design paid.

**Closures carry their constants.** DSpark was closed at 1.03x and then 1.20x because a verify forward costs 242.6 ms + 26.9 ms per extra position (the only multi-position path was prefill, README roadmap item 5)
and a draft block 22.9 ms, at an internal-drive miss cost. Rapid-MLX measured **2.02x** with the same draft family (K = 4, mean accepted 1.85, matching our measured 1.85) on an M3 Ultra once the verify is a
packed decode-shaped forward. The closure was an engineering gap plus a regime, not a wall; and the regime has since changed twice (the bank moved to USB; MiniMax's floor is not what §18.7 assumed).

**Measurement floor.** Process drift +-10 %, the screensaver ~13 %, the Hermes window up to 30 %, memory pressure doubling the non-read part. Levers under ~15 % need per-token alternation in one process.

**A metric that misled us.** `decode_wait_ms_per_miss` shows 1.2 ms where the fitted total is 3.4 ms (MiniMax). Regress ms a token on misses a token, per model, and print it in the stats.

**No common currency for approximations.** Substitution, drops, pruning, bit-sliced reads and a lower-bit tier are judged one session at a time on different texts. One *Pareto harness* (misses a token against NLL/KL
and a checkable battery on fixed traces, with a cache simulator driven by recorded routing) puts them on one curve and is the prerequisite for every output-changing lever below, for all three models.

**Storage is a scarce, shared resource nobody allocated by priority.** Internal SSD: 84 GiB free of 926; holds GLM (169 GiB), MiniMax bank (158 GiB) and its checkpoint, 89 GiB of Library. The X10Pro (500 GB free)
holds DeepSeek's checkpoint (475 GiB) and 2-bit bank (142 GiB) and runs at 1 GB/s. The priority-1 model has the slowest drive. This is the largest single mismatch in the project.
